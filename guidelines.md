# Project Definition

The project should simulate a thesis or a paper, but on a smaller scale. It should be a research project, not an implementation one; thus, your idea must include an innovative component. We encourage you to think about new methods and implementations, and we are happy to support you throughout the ideation process. Please reach out to us (Kai, David, Amir, Kfir) via email to discuss your ideas or initial thoughts about topics you would like to explore further. We are excited to hear about your ideas\!

Once you have an idea for the project, please submit a project proposal via a Piazza private post. You are welcome to discuss it with us briefly before submission. Your proposal should include:

* Project Title & Problem Statement  
* Detailed Description of Idea and Innovation Highlights  
* Implementation Steps   
* Methodology & Datasets, and Models  
* Student IDs & Emails

Make sure to submit early, so we have time to check and respond. **All communication about ideation and submission is via a Piazza private post.**

Make sure to think about resources \- you can’t train a new ChatGPT... Once approved, you can start working on your project and submit it by the deadline. The structure of the report is discussed in the following section.

## Submission 

Please submit a zip folder with the following components:

1. Report \- Please use the following template to write your report: [https://www.overleaf.com/read/pjngxfknwfkj](https://www.overleaf.com/read/pjngxfknwfkj). The internal structure is not mandatory. The maximum number of pages is 8, but shorter reports are welcome. PDF format.  
2. Slides \- Slides summarizing your idea, work, and report. Keep it simple and concise. PDF format.  
3. Presentation \- A 5-minute video (can be shorter), in which you present your slides. Name it as *\[runi\_mail\]\_\[runi\_mail\].zip*

## Grading

Your grade will be based on the following components:

| Percentage | Component |
| :---- | :---- |
| 15% | **Problem definition** \- Is the problem well defined, and is the motivation set correctly? |
| 5% | **Related work-** Did you cover existing approaches to the problem? |
| 20% | **Results and Analysis** \- Do the experiments you chose to run prove your claim? Do they cover everything? Did you discuss and analyze your results? |
| 10% | **Methodology \-** Did you clearly explain what you did and why? |
| 20% | **Novelty** \- Does the project show novelty in framing, implementation, or interpretation? Does it contribute something new, however small, to the field or problem space? |
| 10% | **Report** \- Is the report clear and easy to read? Does it cover all the required components? |
| 5% | **Presentation** \- Is the presentation self-explanatory and easy to read? Does it cover all the required components? Is it well structured and designed? |
| 15% | **General impression** |

If you have any questions or need assistance, don't hesitate to contact us. Good luck, and we look forward to seeing your innovative work\!
