Benchmarking Multi-Model NLP Ensembles for Sensitive-Sentence Classification
Shelly Yaroslavsky  ·  Erel Vanono
Motivating application: email Data Loss Prevention (DLP)
Problem & Motivation

Sensitive information has no single textual form

Structured patterns

Credit-card and social-security numbers follow recognizable formats — regexes catch them.

Irregular / obfuscated forms

The same phone number as a continuous digit run, or split by spaces and hyphens — fixed rules break.

Context-dependent disclosures

Sensitive concepts mentioned in instructions, negations, or hypotheticals — without revealing real data.

Task & Data

Multi-label classification on ai4privacy/pii-masking-300k

benign PII financial confidential
  • Each text may belong to multiple categories; labels are hierarchical — financial and confidential are subsets of PII; benign excludes all.
  • Labels are derived deterministically from the dataset's entity mask (privacy_mask), not independently annotated — this partly explains why pattern-based models are competitive.
  • English subset: 29,908 train / 7,946 test.

Research Questions & Methodology

Four model families, two questions

RQ1: Does a multi-model ensemble outperform the best single model?
RQ2: Does model complementarity vary across sensitivity categories (PII vs. financial vs. confidential)?

Rule-based — pattern-aware

Microsoft Presidio (regex + spaCy NER) + 3 custom recognizers. No training, transparent.

BiLSTM + char-CNN — character-aware

Frozen GloVe + character convolutions → 2-layer BiLSTM. Catches unusual separators & OOV tokens.

BERT & RoBERTa — context-aware

Fine-tuned (last 2 blocks + head) for implicit, instruction-embedded, or hypothetical mentions.

Methodology

Category-specific confidence-weighted late fusion

plensemble(x)  =  Σm wm,l · pm,l(x)  /  Σm wm,l
  • Each model has a different weight per category — e.g., rules for structured financial identifiers, a transformer for context-dependent PII.
  • Weights fit on DEV: per-label grid search refined with Nelder–Mead on per-label F1; decision thresholds tuned on the same DEV split, applied unchanged on TEST.

Reliably positive, practically small.
  • Survives Holm–Bonferroni correction against every member; delta grows as the member weakens (up to +0.172 vs. rule-based).

Summary & Conclusions

What we built, what we found, what it means

What we built

A four-family benchmark — rule-based (Presidio), BiLSTM+char-CNN, BERT, RoBERTa — fused via per-category confidence-weighted late fusion, evaluated on ai4privacy/pii-masking-300k (29 k train / 8 k test).

RQ1: The ensemble reliably beats the best single model (RoBERTa) — macro-F1 +0.0026, p = 0.006, CI [+0.0006, +0.0048]. Reliable, but small.
RQ2: No true per-category specialization — reliance concentrates on RoBERTa across all categories. Removing any other member barely moves the score, which mechanistically explains the small RQ1 margin.
